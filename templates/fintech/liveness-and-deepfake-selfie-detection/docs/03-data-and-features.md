## Data contract

The contract below is a starting point; the owner edits and versions it.

```yaml
contract: liveness_and_deepfake_selfie_detection_inputs.v1
owner: payments-engineering@example.com
system_of_record: Core banking ledger
schema:
  registry_compatibility: BACKWARD_TRANSITIVE
  fields:
    - { name: event_id, type: string, unique: true }
    - { name: event_time, type: timestamp, required: true, tz: UTC }
    - { name: challenge_response_latency, type: double, nullable: false }
    - { name: rppg_pulse_signal, type: double, nullable: false }
    - { name: camera_integrity, type: double, nullable: false }
sla:
  freshness_p95: 60 seconds
  completeness: ">=99.9%"
  duplicates: "<0.01%"
on_breach: { notify: owner, action: quarantine_and_serve_stale, escalate_after: 30m }
```

## Quality SLAs

- **Dimension:** Completeness<br>**Target:** 99.9% of required fields populated<br>**Check:** Null-rate assertion on Challenge response latency and the event key, evaluated per partition
- **Dimension:** Accuracy<br>**Target:** Values reconciled to the system of record daily<br>**Check:** Reconcile aggregates for rPPG pulse signal against Core banking ledger
- **Dimension:** Freshness<br>**Target:** p95 event-to-feature delay 60 seconds<br>**Check:** Measured at the consumer (not the producer) and alerted against the contract
- **Dimension:** Uniqueness<br>**Target:** Duplicate event_id rate below 0.01%<br>**Check:** Idempotent consumers plus a duplicate-rate assertion
- **Dimension:** Validity<br>**Target:** Zero schema violations reach the model plane<br>**Check:** Registry compatibility check in CI; violating rows go to a quarantine table with a reason
- **Dimension:** Point-in-time correctness<br>**Target:** Every training feature is reproducible from data known at decision time<br>**Check:** Replay the logged feature vector for a sample and compare with the offline join

## Lineage, schema change, corrections, privacy

- **Lineage:** Capture source-to-target and column-level lineage (OpenLineage events from every job) so that any served feature, such as Challenge response latency, traces back to Core banking ledger; the decision ledger links each decision to the feature-vector hash and dataset snapshot.
- **On schema change:** Backward-transitive compatibility in the schema registry blocks breaking changes at the producer; undecodable records go to a dead-letter topic and replay through a bounded job once fixed.
- **Corrections and backfills:** Late corrections and backfills write to a versioned namespace at a capped share of store IOPS; a pointer flips only after count and distribution checks pass, and the decision ledger is never rewritten.
- **Privacy and deletion:** Tokenise PAN before it reaches the model plane; purpose-limited use of behavioural data; erasure is key deletion for non-ledger copies while ledger records stay under regulatory retention. Deletion requests propagate by key to feature stores, embeddings and caches; records under statutory retention are sealed rather than deleted, and the exception is recorded.

## Domain knowledge to learn first

A card payment passes through several companies in a few hundred milliseconds, and the fraud decision is made inside that window by whoever you work for: usually the card issuer or a payment service provider. What you can see, what you can do and how long you wait to learn the truth all follow from where you sit in that chain, so learn the chain first. UPI, India's instant account-to-account payment system, works differently: payments are final within seconds and there is no chargeback, so fraud is recovered through complaints and bank-to-bank processes instead. For this use case, "Liveness & Deepfake Selfie Detection": Confirm a live human is present and detect injected or generated faces during remote onboarding, defeating presentation and injection attacks. The terms and ideas below are the ones to learn before you talk to the people who own the data and the decision.

**Concepts**

| Concept | Explanation | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| The cost asymmetry and the operating point | A missed fraud costs the transaction amount plus fees and handling. A false decline costs the margin, a frustrated customer and sometimes the customer. The business chooses a point on the trade-off, expressed as fraud basis points (fraud loss per 10,000 currency units of sales) and a false-decline rate, not as accuracy. | Metrics like accuracy or ROC-AUC mean nothing at a 0.4% fraud rate. Everything is evaluated at the fixed false-positive rate the business agrees in M0. |
| The authorisation flow and where the model sits | The cardholder pays a merchant, the merchant's acquirer or payment service provider sends an authorisation request through the card network to the issuer, and the issuer approves or declines. The fraud model typically runs at the issuer (which sees the card's whole history) or at the PSP and merchant (which see the basket, the device and the checkout session), and each sees different data. | Your feature list is limited to what your position in the chain can see, and the latency budget is a slice of the whole authorisation round trip. |
| Detection, segmentation and measurement | Classification says whether something is present; detection says where; segmentation outlines it pixel by pixel; measurement turns outlines into numbers such as lengths and volumes. Each has its own metrics and tolerances, and real-time use adds latency limits on edge hardware. | Choose the task and metric from the decision: a triage flag needs sensitivity, a surgical plan needs boundary accuracy. |
| Labels, annotators and ground truth in images | Image labels come from expert reads, reports or biopsies and carry noise; annotators disagree, and the boundary of a lesion or defect is a judgment. Class imbalance is extreme: most images are normal. | Measure inter-reader agreement, adjudicate disagreements and report performance against the quality of ground truth, not just a single label. |
| Offline evaluation versus the real world | Offline metrics are computed on historical data; the business cares about outcomes after deployment. The two differ because of leakage, selection bias, delayed labels, distribution shift and feedback loops, which is why launches go through shadow mode and controlled experiments. | Treat every offline number as an estimate with a known direction of bias, and plan the online test before you trust it. |
| This use case's strongest signal: Challenge response latency | Time between prompt and head movement. Real humans respond in 300-800 ms. | Leakage risk to design against: Instrument client-side clocks securely. |
| A signal-versus-noise trap in this use case: Attack type | The true signal is separate heads for print, replay, mask and virtual-camera injection; the common noise is one binary real-vs-fake classifier. | Binary models overfit one attack and miss the next. |

**Glossary**

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Card-not-present (CNP) | A payment made without the physical card, mainly online or by phone, which has a much higher fraud rate than chip-and-PIN. |
| Account takeover (ATO) | A criminal gaining control of a genuine customer's account or card credentials and acting as them. |
| 3-D Secure / SCA | A customer authentication step (3-D Secure) used to meet strong customer authentication rules such as PSD2 in Europe. |
| Fraud basis points (bps) | Fraud losses per 10,000 units of sales value, the standard way to report fraud rates to the business. |
| KYC / CDD | Know your customer and customer due diligence: verifying identity and assessing risk when onboarding and during the relationship. |
| Convolutional network (CNN) | A neural network that learns local image filters shared across the image. |
| IoU | Intersection over union: the overlap between a predicted and a true region divided by their union. |
| mAP | Mean average precision: a detection score averaged over classes and overlap thresholds. |
| Shortcut learning | A model using an easy but irrelevant cue that happens to correlate with the label. |
| Threshold / operating point | The score cut-off at which the system acts, chosen from the cost of errors and capacity. |
| Cost matrix | The money value of each kind of decision error, used to choose thresholds. |
| Train-serve skew | A difference between how features are computed in training and in production. |
| Data leakage | Information in the training data that would not be available at prediction time, making offline results unrealistically good. |
| Point-in-time correctness | Building each training example only from data that existed at the moment of the decision. |
| Distribution shift | A change in the data a model sees compared with training. |
| Concept drift | A change in the relationship between inputs and the outcome over time. |
